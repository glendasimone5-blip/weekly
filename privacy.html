Privacy policy

Last updated: 22 October 2026

The short version

  • Your plans, notes, places and any period or pregnancy information stay on your own device. We cannot see them.
  • There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Two optional features send limited data to our server: sync (encrypted, so we cannot read it) and reminders when the app is closed (the times only, never what they say).
  • You can erase everything at any time.

1. Who is responsible

Weekly is run by Glenda Pringle (“we”). You can contact us at glendasimone5@gmail.com.

2. What stays on your device

Weekly stores the following in your browser’s own storage on the device you use. We do not receive it.

  • Tasks, events (including times and places you type), categories, notes, subtasks and reminders.
  • If you choose to turn it on: period, cycle and pregnancy information.
  • Your settings, a small log of technical problems (kept on the device, never sent), and, if you use them, your sync code and app-lock details.

This storage is strictly necessary to make the app work. Weekly sets no advertising or tracking cookies. If you turn on the app lock, this stored data is encrypted with a passphrase only you know. Clearing your browser’s data for this site erases it.

When your device contacts our hosting (GitHub, which serves the app’s files) or our server (Cloudflare), those companies process your IP address and technical details, as any web service does. See their own privacy notices.

4. Other services your actions may use

  • Address search. Only when you press the search button on an event, the text you typed and your IP address are sent to OpenStreetMap’s search service. Results are © OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Map links. If you click a location link, your device opens Google Maps (or your maps app) with that place.
  • Notifications. Reminders are delivered through your browser maker’s push service (for example Google, Microsoft, Apple or Mozilla). Our signal contains no text. With the app lock on, notifications show only “Weekly reminder”.

Weekly loads no outside scripts, fonts or images: the chart, image and font files are served from this site.

5. Health information

Period, cycle and pregnancy tracking is optional and off by default. When you turn it on, we ask for your explicit consent. The information stays on your device; it only leaves it if you choose to use encrypted sync or save a backup file. The estimates it shows are based on averages. They are not medical advice and must not be used as contraception. You can switch the feature off, and erase the data, at any time.

6. Why we use data

  • To provide the features you switch on (sync, reminders, address search).
  • To keep the service secure and stop misuse (our legitimate interest).
  • For health information, your explicit consent, which you give by turning the feature on and can withdraw by turning it off.

7. Keeping data safe

Sync copies are encrypted on your device (AES-256) before they are sent. Connections use HTTPS. The app can only connect to our own server and the address service. You can add an app lock and encrypted backups. Please look after your sync code, passphrases and devices: we cannot recover a lost code or passphrase. No system is perfectly secure.

8. Children

Weekly is meant for people aged 13 or over. If you are under 18, please use it with a parent or guardian’s knowledge. It has no accounts, no chat, no public profiles and no advertising.

9. Where data is processed

GitHub and Cloudflare operate worldwide, so the limited data described in section 3 may be processed outside the UK. They use recognised safeguards for such transfers.

10. Your rights

Under UK data protection law you may ask to see, correct or delete your personal data, restrict or object to its use, receive a copy, and withdraw consent. Most of your data is on your own device, so you control it directly. For anything on our server, you can delete it yourself (turn sync off and choose to delete) or write to us. Because we cannot read synced data or link it to you without your code, we may ask you to use that route. You may also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk).

11. Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date above and, for significant changes, tell you in the app.
